# Original decode report

A numbers-and-structure record of what was decoded from a user-supplied copy of the 1991 DOS release that the `classic` ruleset is modelled on, compared with this repository. It is evidence for tuning decisions, not a specification, and it stays under the IP rules in PRD 2.6: no names, text, art, audio or code from the original appear here, and none may be copied into the game. Field names below are neutral descriptions, not the original identifiers.

Related: `docs/ORIGINAL_REFERENCE.md` (the researched baseline), ADR-0069 (audited deviations), ADR-0070 (clothing), ADR-0071 (why this report is checked in).

## 1. Method and confidence

- The game ships as a compiled script archive plus a small interpreter, so the rules live in script bytecode and object data, not in the executable. A purpose-built reader parsed the archive (813 resources, stored uncompressed), recovered the 500 property and method names, parsed script objects and their property tables, and disassembled methods from their real entry points.
- **Data** rows (tables read directly from object property values or local-variable blocks) are high confidence.
- **Code** rows (read from disassembled methods) are high confidence when the logic is short and self-checking, and noted where they are not.
- **Inferred** rows are plausible readings that were not independently confirmed.
- Nothing was run or emulated; all findings are static.

## 4. Differences to act on

Each needs its own ADR and a `sim:gate` run before any change.

1. Burglary: switch to `1 / (relaxation + 1)`, independent of durables.
2. Loans: add the base-game loan to classic (4-week cycle, $100 units) once the terms are confirmed.
3. Career stat: stepwise `floor(dep/8) x 10`; compare thresholds before changing.
4. Extra study credit: books as a set (1) plus the computer (1).
5. Start relaxation 25 versus 10.
6. Item catalogue: add stove, black-and-white television, baseball tickets; revisit the hot-tub, encyclopedia and refrigerator prices; lengthen outfit durations.
7. Job table: 38 jobs on a ten-step requirement ladder versus our 46; fewer two-degree jobs.
8. Lottery: prize tiers 200 / 500 / 5000 with a single resolution per week and a `q/501` win chance, instead of per-ticket rolls that pay about four times the stake.
9. Weekend and doctor costs: the original draws costs from ranges that grow with the week and shrink when cash is low (doctor capped at 50 below 500 cash, never above cash on hand); ours are fixed per event. The computer pays 20-100 with a 1-in-7 weekly chance; ours pays 50-150 at weight 10.
10. Market and economy: replace per-instrument bounded walks with the original's shared trend model (rules 26-31) if a faithful classic is wanted; the crash and boom odds depend on a game setting that is not yet identified.
11. Weekend events: replace the 12 weighted events with the original's structure (ticket event, then a uniform draw from 42 events in three cost tiers, no immediate repeats, a single positive-happiness event) if a faithful classic is wanted.
12. Street thief: flat 1-in-51 (grocery) and 1-in-31 (bank) per entry from week 4, taking all cash on hand, instead of a rate that rises with cash carried.
13. Experience cap: `jobExperienceRequirement + 10` (plus 10 for entry jobs) with +2 experience on hire, instead of `20 + requirement + 5 per degree`.
14. Firing margin 5 with no happiness penalty, and rent-office opening weeks plus apartment access control.
15. Food cost: weekly food at 55 (grocery) or 65-124 (burger place) base, scaled by the price level, against our 15 per unit; this is the largest cost difference found and is likely to change early-game balance if aligned.
16. Wages, pawn prices and share prices all move with the price level in the original; ours are fixed. T-Bills carry a flat 3 sale fee, other trades have no fee.
17. Eviction: the original has none; unpaid rent only builds debt and garnishes wages. Rent extension odds are 100%, 75%, 50% then 25% by prior extensions, and moving grants no happiness.

## 7. Rival decision rules

The computer opponent is a priority-ordered rule list, not a search. Each turn it resumes at a rule index (so it can fall through earlier rules), picks the first rule whose conditions hold, and returns a destination on the ring plus up to three actions to perform on arrival, a quantity, and a plan id. Every plan that involves earning, withdrawing or travelling first checks that route time plus work time fits in the hours left (`used + needed <= 60`). Route time is the sum of path-point distances between stops (capped at half the 172-point ring) at 15 points per hour, plus 2 hours per stop. Prices are base prices scaled by the economy reading. Confidence is medium: the action codes were not mapped one to one.

Rules in priority order, most urgent first:

1. Unemployed and not already refused this week: go to the employment office to apply.
2. No food, outfit lasts more than a week, cash covers rent plus food: buy food (grocery if a refrigerator is owned, otherwise the burger place), then pay rent.
3. No food, cash covers rent plus outfit plus food: buy food, buy an outfit, pay rent.
4. Cash covers an outfit and the outfit is about to expire: buy an outfit.
5. No food, cash under rent but covers food: buy food only.
6. Cash covers rent: pay rent at the rent office.
7. Employed and dressed, rent short: work `ceil((rent - cash) / (8 x wage))` sessions if the route and work time fit in the week.
8. Cash plus bank covers rent and the bank is reachable in time: withdraw, then pay rent.
9. Not yet asked this month and cash plus three weeks of wages covers rent: ask for an extension.
10. Pawnable durables plus cash cover rent: pawn the items to raise the shortfall.
11. The same chain repeats for an outfit shortfall (work, withdraw, pawn, buy) and for a food shortfall (work, withdraw, pawn, buy).
12. Career: ask for a raise if raises are under 2, dependability is at least the job minimum plus 10 plus 5 per raise, and the current market wage exceeds the paid wage; look for a better job if experience has reached its cap (cap under 80), dependability is at least the job minimum plus 10, and the top wage exceeds the current wage; work extra sessions to close the gap to the experience cap or the dependability requirement.
13. When employed, dressed and allowed to spend: redeem pawned items when cash covers the redemption price; go home to relax when relaxation is 10, or below 17; enrol in a course with a 1-in-2 chance when cash is over 400 and degrees are under 11; buy durables or reference books when cash is over 800 or 1500, skipping purchases once the happiness goal is met; buy and sell market holdings when the economy trend is up or down and cash or bank balances pass 750 and 1500.
14. Fallback: go home.

What this gives a rebuild: the rival's behaviour is a survival-first planner (food, outfit, rent), then career, then study, then optional spending, with fixed cash thresholds of 400, 750, 800 and 1500 and a 1-in-2 coin for study. Exact conditions for the market rules and the action codes remain unmapped.
